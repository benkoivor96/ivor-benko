What to pack for a week of sailing in Croatia

21 September 20262 min readWritten by Ivor Benko

Short answer

Pack light in a soft bag, bring non-marking shoes, strong sun protection, a warm layer and a windproof jacket for the evenings, a power bank and your documents. Leave the hard suitcase and most of your wardrobe at home.

Storage space on a sailboat is limited, and the Croatian summer is simpler than people expect. This is the list I send to my guests.

The bag

Bring a soft bag, not a hard suitcase. Hard suitcases don’t fit into cabin lockers and there’s nowhere to keep them. A duffel bag folds flat and disappears.

Clothes

  • swimwear, at least two sets, so one is always dry
  • light shorts and T-shirts
  • a long-sleeved shirt that blocks the sun
  • a warm layer for the evenings, because it gets cool on the water, especially if bura blows
  • a light windproof and waterproof jacket
  • something nicer for dinner in town

That’s enough. You’ll wear far less than you pack.

Shoes

  • deck shoes or trainers with light, non-marking soles, because black soles leave marks on the deck
  • sandals for towns
  • water shoes if you like rocky beaches, since sea urchins are common on the rocks

Sun and heat

The sun on the water is stronger than on land, because it also reflects off the sea.

  • sunscreen with high protection, and plenty of it
  • a hat that won’t blow away, or one with a strap
  • polarised sunglasses with a strap
  • lip balm with SPF
  • a refillable water bottle

Health

  • seasickness remedies if you’re prone to it, taken before you sail, not after you feel ill
  • your regular medicines, plasters and something for small cuts
  • after-sun

Electronics

  • a power bank and USB cables; most boats have USB sockets, but there are never enough
  • a waterproof phone pouch or a small dry bag
  • a headlamp or small torch for evenings at anchor

Documents and money

  • ID card or passport for everyone
  • your boat licence and VHF certificate, if you’re sailing without a skipper
  • a bank card, plus some cash for small konobas, bakeries and mooring buoys in smaller bays

Nice to have

  • snorkel and mask
  • a quick-dry towel for the beach, though boats usually come with towels, so check with the charter company
  • a book, cards or a small speaker at a considerate volume

Leave at home

The hard suitcase, high heels, hair dryers (most boats can’t power them away from the marina) and more than one “just in case” outfit.

If you’re sailing with me, I’ll send you a short list tailored to the boat and the season before you arrive. Here’s how I work as a skipper.
