Do you need a skipper for a sailing holiday in Croatia?

31 August 20263 min readWritten by Ivor Benko

Short answer

You need a skipper in Croatia if nobody in your group holds a boat licence recognised in Croatia plus a VHF radio certificate. Even with licences, many groups hire one to handle marinas, anchoring and weather, so that everyone else can actually be on holiday.

I get this question from almost every group that books a charter for the first time. The honest answer has two parts: what the rules say, and what the week will feel like.

What the rules say

To take a charter yacht out without a professional, at least one person on board has to hold a boat licence recognised in Croatia, and in practice also a VHF radio operator certificate (often called SRC). Charter companies check both documents at check-in, before they hand over the boat.

If nobody in your group has them, you book the boat “with skipper”. The charter company either assigns one or you bring your own, like me.

What a skipper actually does

A skipper is responsible for the boat and for the safety of everyone on it. In a normal week that means:

  • Planning the route around the weather. In the Adriatic, a good route is decided by the forecast, not by a list of famous places. I check it every morning and evening and adjust the plan.
  • Marinas, buoys and anchoring. Mediterranean mooring (stern-to with lazy lines) in a busy marina in July is the most stressful part of most charters. That is my job.
  • Boat check-in and check-out. Going through the inventory, the engine, the sails and the damage report with the charter base, so you are not surprised at the end of the week.
  • Local knowledge. Which bays are protected from which wind, where you can tie up if you eat at the restaurant, where the water is clearest in the morning.
  • Teaching, if you want it. Many guests want to steer, trim sails or learn to tie the essential knots. I worked as a sailing instructor, so I’m happy to turn the week into a course.

What a skipper usually doesn’t do

A skipper is not a cook or a cleaner. Some crews share cooking with the skipper, some eat out every night, but by default meal preparation and cleaning the guest cabins are not part of the job. If you want that, ask the charter company about a hostess.

Even if you have a licence

A licence proves you passed an exam. It doesn’t tell you how the maestral builds on a July afternoon near Split, or which anchorage will be uncomfortable if the wind turns overnight. Groups with a licensed friend often hire a skipper anyway, so that the friend can also have a holiday.

What to agree on before the trip

  • Skipper fee and food. The skipper’s fee is usually paid separately from the boat, and guests normally cover the skipper’s meals on board. Confirm both with the charter company or with the skipper directly.
  • Where the skipper sleeps. Usually a separate cabin or the skipper’s cabin that many catamarans have in the bow. Settle this before you choose the boat.
  • The rough plan. Tell your skipper what you want: long sailing days, quiet anchorages, towns and nightlife, or a mix. A good skipper then builds the week around the weather.
  • Language. Make sure you can communicate easily. I sail in Croatian, English and German.

The short version

If you have no licence, you need a skipper. If you have one but want the week to feel like a holiday, a skipper is still the easiest upgrade you can make. If you’re planning a charter in Croatia and would like me on board, see how I work as a skipper.
