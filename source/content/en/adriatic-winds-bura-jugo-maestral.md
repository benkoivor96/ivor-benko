Every sailing week in Croatia is planned around one question: which wind is coming? Once you know the three main winds, the Adriatic becomes much easier to read.

## Maestral: the summer sailing wind

Maestral is a northwesterly thermal breeze. On a typical summer day the morning is calm, the breeze fills in around late morning or midday, peaks in the afternoon and dies down around sunset.

It is the wind most charter guests remember fondly: steady, predictable and usually moderate. It means fair weather. A classic summer routine is to motor or swim in the calm morning, sail in the maestral after lunch, and reach the evening anchorage before it drops.

## Bura: fast, cold and gusty

Bura (bora) blows from the northeast, down from the mountains along the coast towards the sea. It is dry and cold, and it often comes with a clear blue sky, which is exactly why it catches people out.

What makes bura dangerous is not only its strength but its gusts. It is gusty and uneven, and it is strongest in certain areas below the coastal mountains, such as the Velebit Channel. In summer it is usually shorter and weaker than in winter, but even a summer bura can make an exposed anchorage unusable within an hour.

Signs I watch for:

- a forecast for a northeasterly, even a "moderate" one
- a band of cloud sitting on the mountain tops along the coast, locally called a cap
- a sudden drop in temperature and very clear air

When bura is forecast, I choose anchorages and harbours protected to the north and east, and I don't plan open-water crossings under the mountains.

## Jugo: warm, wet and building

Jugo blows from the southeast. It is warm and humid, and it builds gradually, often over a day or two. It runs along the whole length of the Adriatic, so it has space to raise a long, heavy sea.

Jugo is more common outside the peak summer months, but it can appear in any season. It often brings haze, low cloud and rain. Signs:

- falling barometric pressure
- humid, heavy air and poor visibility
- swell from the southeast before the wind itself arrives

In jugo, bays open to the south and southeast become uncomfortable or dangerous, and crossing between islands against it is slow and wet.

## Other winds you'll hear about

- **Burin** is a light night breeze blowing from land to sea, the night-time counterpart to maestral.
- **Tramontana** comes from the north. It is cooler and can be strong.
- **Lebić (garbin)** comes from the southwest. It is less common but can be short and violent, and it hits harbours that are otherwise well protected.

## Which forecasts to use

I use the official forecasts of the Croatian Meteorological and Hydrological Service (DHMZ), including the maritime forecast and its model-based wind maps, and compare them with global apps. Coastal radio stations broadcast weather reports on VHF, and marina offices usually post the forecast every day.

The rule I work by is simple: check the forecast in the morning and in the evening, and choose tonight's anchorage for tomorrow's wind, not today's.
