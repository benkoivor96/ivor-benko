Kornati is where I spent a lot of my early time at sea. I worked as a sailing instructor on Murter, the island right next door, so these islands feel a little like home.

## What the Kornati are

The Kornati National Park was founded in 1980 and covers 89 islands, islets and reefs. The landscape is unlike anywhere else on the Croatian coast: bare, pale stone islands with almost no trees, dry-stone walls running across the hills, and very clear water. On the seaward side, many islands end in steep cliffs, locally called "krune" (crowns).

It is quiet. There are no towns inside the park, only a few seasonal houses and restaurants in the coves.

## Where to start

The closest bases are on Murter, including the villages of Murter, Jezera and Betina, all within a few hours of sailing. Charters from the Šibenik and Zadar areas also reach the Kornati easily, and Telašćica Nature Park on Dugi otok lies at the northwestern end of the archipelago.

## Before you enter the park

- **Buy the park ticket.** Every boat entering the park needs a ticket. It is sold online and at reception points, and it is usually cheaper to buy in advance than inside the park.
- **Fill up water and fuel.** There is very little of either inside the park. Leave with full tanks.
- **Know the rules.** Anchoring, fishing and diving are restricted in some zones, and there are strictly protected areas. The rules are published by the park and change from time to time, so check them before the trip.

## Weather in the Kornati

The islands are low and bare, so they offer little protection from the wind itself, only from the waves. That makes choosing the right cove for the night especially important:

- in maestral, pick coves open to the southeast and closed to the northwest
- in bura, pick coves protected to the northeast
- in jugo, avoid bays open to the south and southeast

I explain the three winds in more detail in [bura, jugo and maestral explained](/blog/adriatic-winds-bura-jugo-maestral/).

## How I like to sail the Kornati

A good Kornati day has no fixed plan. A morning swim in a still bay, a sail between the islands in the afternoon breeze, a walk up to a viewpoint above the cove, and dinner at one of the konobas where you can tie up if you eat there. Give the park at least one full day, ideally two, with a night anchored inside.

## Combine it with

- **Telašćica** and the saltwater lake Mir on Dugi otok
- the **Krka** river estuary and Skradin, if your base is near Šibenik
- the small islands of the Šibenik archipelago on the way back
